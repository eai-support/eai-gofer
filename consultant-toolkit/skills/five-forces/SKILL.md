# Five Forces

Explain how attractive an industry is, and why, using Porter's Five Forces.

## Input

- The industry, defined by product scope and geography.

## Method

1. Define the industry boundary first. A wrong boundary gives a wrong answer.
2. Assess each force with its drivers:
   - Rivalry: number and size of rivals, growth, fixed costs, exit barriers, differentiation.
   - Threat of new entrants: scale economies, capital needs, access to channels, regulation, brand.
   - Threat of substitutes: price and performance of alternatives, switching costs.
   - Buyer power: concentration, volume, switching costs, price sensitivity, backward integration.
   - Supplier power: concentration, uniqueness of inputs, switching costs, forward integration.
3. Rate each force Low, Medium, or High, with its trend (rising, stable, falling).
4. Note complementors and regulation where they shape profits.
5. Conclude on overall attractiveness and where profit pools sit in the value chain.

## Output

1. Table: Force | Key drivers and evidence | Rating | Trend | Implication.
2. Overall attractiveness verdict and profit-pool notes.

## Quality bar

- Every rating is backed by drivers and evidence.
- Implications say what the client should do differently.

## Boundaries

- Not for macro trends: use `pestel-scan`.
- Not for comparing named competitors: use `competitor-benchmark`.
- Hand off to: `swot-analysis`, `strategic-options`.

## Rules

- If a required input is missing, ask once when you can. Otherwise state an assumption, label it [Assumption], and continue.
- Do not invent facts or figures. Label estimates [Estimate] and cite every source.
