# Data Storytelling

Explain what one analysis means, in a sequence a non-analyst can follow.

## Input

- One analysis or dataset, its result, and the audience.

## Method

1. Write the big idea: one sentence with a point of view and what is at stake.
2. Name the audience question the data answers.
3. Choose the comparison that proves the idea: against target, prior period, peers, or plan.
4. Add context: baseline, benchmark, and scale.
5. Remove data that does not serve the idea.
6. Plan emphasis: highlight one series or point, mute the rest, and annotate the moment that matters.
7. Arrange beats: setup (what is normal), tension (what changed), resolution (what it means and what to do).
8. Say numbers plainly: round them, use relatable ratios ("1 in 4"), keep units consistent, avoid false precision.

## Output

1. Big idea.
2. Beat table: Beat | Data point or exhibit | What to say | What to highlight.
3. Caption or narration text.

## Quality bar

- One big idea, stated before the data.
- Each beat uses one comparison only.

## Boundaries

- Not for choosing the chart type: use `chart-selection`.
- Not for the whole deck structure: use `recommendation-deck`.
- Not for combining many workstreams: use `insight-synthesis`.

## Rules

- If a required input is missing, ask once when you can. Otherwise state an assumption, label it [Assumption], and continue.
- Do not invent facts or figures. Label estimates [Estimate] and cite every source.
