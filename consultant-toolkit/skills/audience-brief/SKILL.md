# Audience Brief

Define who will receive one specific deliverable and what they need from it, so content and format fit the reader.

## Input

- The deliverable type, the occasion (pre-read, live meeting, email), and the people who will receive it.

## Method

1. Name the primary decision maker and the key influencers in the room.
2. State the action you need from them after this deliverable.
3. Record what they already know and believe about the topic.
4. Record what they care about most: metrics, risks, reputation, timing.
5. List likely objections and the evidence that answers each one.
6. Set the format: length, level of detail, data depth, tone, language, and whether the answer comes first.
7. Note sensitivities: words, people, or numbers to handle with care.

## Output

A one-page brief with these fields: Audience, Action needed, Current knowledge and beliefs, Priorities, Likely objections and answers, Format and length, Tone, Sensitivities.

## Quality bar

- One required action is named.
- Every objection has a planned answer.
- Format choices follow from the audience, not habit.

## Boundaries

- Not for mapping everyone affected by a change: use `stakeholder-map`.
- Not for defining the question itself: use `problem-framing`.
- Hand off to: `scqa-storyline`, `recommendation-deck`, `executive-summary`, `decision-memo`, `speaker-notes`.

## Rules

- If a required input is missing, ask once when you can. Otherwise state an assumption, label it [Assumption], and continue.
- Do not invent facts, figures, or sources. Use provided material or sources you can check; label other knowledge [Unverified] and estimates [Estimate].
