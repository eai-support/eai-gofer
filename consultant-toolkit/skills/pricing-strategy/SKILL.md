# Pricing Strategy

Recommend how to charge, how much, and how to protect price in the market.

## Input

- The offer, target segments, competitor prices, unit costs, and pricing goals (share, margin, positioning).

## Method

1. Set the pricing objective.
2. Estimate value to the customer: price of the next best alternative plus the value of differences.
3. Estimate willingness to pay. Name the method used: interviews, price-sensitivity survey (Van Westendorp), conjoint, or market tests.
4. Choose the price metric: per unit, per user, by usage, by outcome, or subscription.
5. Design the structure: good-better-best tiers, bundles, and add-ons.
6. Set the price level between the cost floor and the value ceiling, with reference to competitors.
7. Set discount rules: limits, approval levels, and terms.
8. Estimate the effect on volume, revenue, and margin. State the elasticity assumption.
9. Plan rollout: communication, existing customers, and monitoring.

## Output

1. Pricing recommendation table: Element | Recommendation | Rationale.
2. Price and tier table.
3. Impact estimate with assumptions.

## Quality bar

- Price is anchored to customer value, not only cost.
- Discount rules are specific and enforceable.

## Boundaries

- Not for unit costs and margins: use `unit-economics`.
- Not for defining segments: use `customer-segmentation`.
- Not for competitor price research: use `competitor-benchmark`.

## Rules

- If a required input is missing, ask once when you can. Otherwise state an assumption, label it [Assumption], and continue.
- Do not invent facts or figures. Label estimates [Estimate] and cite every source.
