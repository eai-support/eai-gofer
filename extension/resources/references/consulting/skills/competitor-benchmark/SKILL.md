# Competitor Benchmark

Show where the client stands against named peers, and what drives the gaps.

## Input

- The client, a peer set, and the comparison questions.

## Method

1. Choose the peer set: direct rivals, indirect rivals, and one best-in-class reference.
2. Choose metrics that answer the question: financial (growth, margin), operational (cost per unit, productivity), customer (share, NPS, price), capability (digital, footprint, products).
3. Normalize: same definitions, fiscal year, currency, and per-unit basis. Note any gaps.
4. Fill a client vs. peers table. Show quartile or rank.
5. For each major gap, explain the likely driver and what peers do differently.

## Output

1. Benchmark table: Metric | Definition | Client | Peer A | Peer B | Best-in-class | Client rank | Source.
2. Top 3 to 5 gaps with drivers and implications.

## Quality bar

- Like-for-like definitions, or a stated caveat.
- Every figure has a source and a date.

## Boundaries

- Not for industry-level forces: use `five-forces`.
- Not for setting price: use `pricing-strategy`.
- Not for rating source credibility: use `source-validation`.

## Rules

- If a required input is missing, ask once when you can. Otherwise state an assumption, label it [Assumption], and continue.
- Do not invent facts, figures, or sources. Use provided material or sources you can check; label other knowledge [Unverified] and estimates [Estimate].
